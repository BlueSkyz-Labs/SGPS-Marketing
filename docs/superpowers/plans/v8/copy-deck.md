**Findings that change the plan (contradiction check).** (1) NN/g and GOV.UK both favour plain, objective copy; none of the sources supports brand-style abstract taglines on a first screen, which is consistent with the Owner complaint. (2) Practitioner advice on pre-launch pages favours a waitlist, which conflicts with this site's no-forms, no-cookies privacy claim; I side with the repo truth and flag it. (3) The AI-word lists are blog-grade; I use them only to name patterns, while GOV.UK is the authority for word choice. (4) The Owner-locked H1 contains three flagged words ("intelligent", "empower", "elevate"); the deck keeps it and compensates with specificity beside it.

### Copy rules applied (derived, 10)

1. Lead with who and what, then the outcome; one idea per sentence.
2. Show a fact instead of asserting a quality ("no cookies" not "clear data boundaries").
3. Every claim once per page, with its limit and sources next to it.
4. One primary CTA per view; verb plus object; under five words; name the product in repeated links.
5. Status first for in-development products ("In development: …"); no roadmap, no next stage, no dates beyond sourced capture/review dates.
6. No contractions, idioms or metaphors in new EN copy; avoid "empower/elevate/seamless/intelligent/unlock/robust/leverage".
7. Visitors never meet internal terms (SGPS, Atlas, Truth Gate, dossier, salon, ladder, node ids, revision hashes).
8. Omit any fact without a repo source; never invent metrics, users, dates, partners, locations or emails.
9. Sign-in is for existing users: text link, labelled as such; never the primary action on an in-development product.
10. Write for expansion: short strings, one term per concept per locale.
